Margin of safety
Coal India is the world's largest coal miner — a government monopoly with 35% ROCE, zero debt, and a 5.7% dividend yield at just 9x earnings. The valuation screams cheap. But India's energy transition from coal to renewables is real and accelerating. This is an income vehicle for 5–7 years, not a 15-year compounder. ⛏️
Business Overview
What they do and why it's hard to displace

Coal India Limited (CIL) is the world's largest coal producer, a Government of India Maharatna PSU. It produces ~700 million tonnes annually across 83 mines operated by 8 subsidiaries. ~80% of offtake goes to thermal power plants at regulated prices; the balance via e-auction at market rates.

No private competitor can displace it — it controls India's entire domestic coal supply chain. But India's renewable energy ambitions are the structural long-term risk to this dominance.

Competitive Moat: Government Monopoly + Irreplaceable National Infrastructure

Strength: Wide — but structurally time-limited. The moat is not built on innovation or customer loyalty; it is built on government policy. When that policy changes, the moat erodes with it.

Key Risks
The energy transition is the existential question
Energy Transition — Renewable Displacement
HIGH
India's renewables scaling 15%+ annually. New coal power is already more expensive than firm renewables on a tariff basis. By FY2031, coal tariffs will be ~25% costlier than solar+wind. This is the existential 10-year risk — not if, but when, coal demand peaks.
Government Divestment / PSU Discount
HIGH
India plans to sell ~INR 8,800 crore of CIL stake, creating a supply overhang on the stock. As a PSU, CIL also trades at a structural governance discount vs. private peers regardless of how strong the fundamentals are.
E-Auction Price Compression
MEDIUM
E-auction premiums are compressing as global coal prices soften. Operating margin fell from 32.5% in FY2024 to 18.5% in FY2026. If global prices remain weak, this margin pressure continues.
Wage & Cost Inflation
MEDIUM
240,000+ employees. Court-mandated FY2026 pay hike added INR 1,458Cr one-time provision. Structural wage inflation is partially outside management control and erodes operating leverage over time.
Production Volume Shortfalls
LOW
CIL has historically missed production targets. The 810MT FY2028 guidance requires a sustained 5% volume CAGR. If India's power demand disappoints or renewables scale faster than expected, this target could be missed.

VERDICT: WATCH

Coal India is a fantastic income vehicle for the next 5–7 years. It is not a 15-year compounder. The business will likely peak in FY2028–30 and face secular volume decline thereafter as India's renewable capacity scales.

For income investors: The 5.7% yield, zero debt, and government backing make this a bond-like instrument with equity upside. At INR 380–430, it is an excellent income buy.
