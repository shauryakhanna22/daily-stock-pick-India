## 1. Business Overview

Coal India Limited (CIL) is the world's largest coal mining company, a Government of India Maharatna PSU under the Ministry of Coal. It produces and supplies coal primarily to India's thermal power sector (accounting for ~80% of offtake) and operates 83 mines across 8 subsidiaries. Revenue is ~INR 1,68,400 crore (FY2026), nearly entirely from domestic coal sales at regulated and e-auction prices.

**Moat type:** Government-backed monopoly + irreplaceable national infrastructure
**Moat strength:** Wide — but structurally time-limited

The moat is unusual: Coal India doesn't compete in a free market. It is a state-owned monopoly with captive demand from India's power sector. No private company can easily displace it. However, this moat is not built on innovation or customer loyalty — it is built on government policy. When that policy changes (and it will, as renewables scale), the moat erodes with it.

| FY2023 | 204 | 318 | **0.64x** | ⚠️ Moderate |

**Earnings quality rating: MEDIUM — improving but inconsistent**

The FY2024 and FY2025 FCF/NI ratios are deeply concerning — in FY2024, the company generated INR 374B net income but only INR 13B in free cash flow. The gap is explained by heavy capital expenditure on mine development and infrastructure, plus significant working capital swings (receivables from state electricity boards, which are notoriously slow payers). FY2026 marks a strong recovery with FCF/NI at 0.99x — the best in 4 years. The pattern suggests earnings quality is capex-cycle dependent.

**One-time item flag:** In Q3 FY2026, Coal India recognized a INR 1,457.90 crore provision for executive salary arrears (August 2023–December 2025) following a High Court order. This is a genuine one-time charge that depressed FY2026 PAT. Stripping it out, underlying PAT would have been higher.

**EBITDA margin compression:** EBITDA margin contracted 584 bps from Q2 FY2025 to Q2 FY2026 — driven by cost inflation in wages, overburden removal, and lower e-auction realizations. This is the key near-term earnings risk.

## 3. Earnings Call / Management Signals

**FY2026 Key Management Points:**
- Full-year PAT of INR 31,071 crore — down 12.4% YoY — management attributed this to lower e-auction volumes and one-time salary provision
- Q4 FY2026 PAT of INR 10,908 crore — strong recovery vs. Q4 FY2025 (INR 9,740 crore) — positive sequential trend
- Final dividend of INR 5.25/share declared for FY2026, maintaining strong capital return commitment
- Production volume guidance: dispatch expected to reach 810 million tonnes by FY2028 (5% CAGR)
- Management tone: **Cautious-Confident** — acknowledged margin pressures but pointed to demand recovery

**Key analyst concerns from results commentary:**
- Why are e-auction premiums compressing? (Softer global coal prices reducing arbitrage)
- How does CIL plan to protect margins as wages rise? (Limited pricing flexibility under government oversight)
- What is the impact of government divestment plans? (India likely to divest ~INR 8,800 crore stake — supply overhang on the stock)

**Guidance tone:** Moderate. Volume growth is real; margin recovery is uncertain.

| Governance | PSU — subject to ministry directives | ⚠️ Not market-driven decisions |
| Divestment risk | India plans INR 8,800 crore stake sale | ⚠️ Supply overhang |

**Management quality rating: ADEQUATE**

Coal India is a PSU — management is government-appointed and subject to Ministry of Coal directives. Decisions on pricing, capacity expansion, and dividends are influenced by policy as much as by business logic. The government's 63.1% stake means the "promoter" is the Indian state — which has incentives to extract dividends (fiscal revenue) but also to keep coal affordable (energy security mandate). This dual mandate is the central governance risk for CIL as a long-term investment.

The consistent high dividend payout (INR 15.5/share in FY2026 across interim and final dividends) shows capital returns remain a priority. But strategic decisions are not purely shareholder-value driven.

## 8. Key Risks (Top 5)

| # | Risk | Severity | Commentary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Energy transition — renewable displacement** | HIGH | India's renewable capacity is growing at 15%+ annually. By FY2031, solar + wind could displace meaningful thermal coal demand. New coal plants already uneconomic vs. renewables on tariff basis. This is the existential 10-year risk for CIL. |
| 2 | **Government divestment / PSU discount** | HIGH | India plans to sell ~INR 8,800 crore of CIL stake. Supply overhang depresses stock price. Also, as a PSU, CIL trades at a structural discount vs. private sector peers regardless of fundamentals. |
| 3 | **E-auction price compression** | MEDIUM | E-auction premiums (which generate higher margins than regulated prices) have been compressing as global coal prices soften. If e-auction realizations fall, margins compress further beyond FY2026 levels. |
| 4 | **Wage and cost inflation** | MEDIUM | Coal India employs ~240,000+ workers. Wage revisions (like the FY2026 court-mandated pay hike) add significant fixed costs. Mining cost inflation is structural and partially beyond management control. |
| 5 | **Production volume shortfalls** | LOW-MEDIUM | CIL has historically missed production targets. If India's power demand growth disappoints or renewable scaling accelerates faster than expected, volume growth could be below the 5% CAGR guidance. |

## 9. Bull / Base / Bear Cases

### Bull (~+127% over 5 years — INR 1,061)
- India's power demand grows faster than expected (6%+ CAGR), keeping coal baseload demand elevated through FY2032
- E-auction premium recovery as global coal prices stabilize
- Government accelerates CIL capex into higher-value coking coal / coal chemicals
- Re-rating of PSU discount as governance reforms improve

### Base (~+47% over 5 years — INR 685)
- Steady 5% volume CAGR to 810MT by FY2028
- Margins stabilize at 20–22% operating margin
- Dividends continue at INR 15–18/share annually (5%+ yield on cost)
- No meaningful ESG-driven institutional selling

### Bear (~–17% over 5 years — INR 388)
- Renewable scaling accelerates faster than consensus → thermal coal demand peaks by FY2028
- E-auction volumes fall sharply → margin compression to <15% operating margin
- Government forced divestment at discount prices → stock re-rates lower

## Investment Conclusion

**Coal India is a fantastic income vehicle, a mediocre long-term compounding vehicle.**

- **For income investors:** The 5.7% dividend yield, zero debt, and government backing make this a bond-like instrument with equity upside. At INR 380–430, it is an excellent income buy.
- **For long-term compounders:** The energy transition risk makes this unsuitable as a 15–20 year hold. The business will likely peak in FY2028–30 and face secular volume decline thereafter.
- **Verdict: WATCH** — wait for a dip to INR 380–430 to create adequate margin of safety, then hold for the next 5–7 years collecting dividends while the energy transition plays out.
