## Market Context
The Nifty 50 closed at 23,650 on May 25, 2026, consolidating in a narrow 23,200–23,900 band as IT stocks led gains (Infosys +2.4%, TechM +4.9%) while macro headwinds — a record-low rupee at ₹96.53/USD, crude oil above $110, and elevated bond yields — weighed on broader sentiment. ### #1 - HDFCBANK (NSE): HDFC Bank Ltd
**Sector:** Banking & Financial Services | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR 11,80,590 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** HDFC Bank trades at just 15.6x earnings and ~2.5x book — a multi-year low premium for India's largest private bank, still digesting its mega-merger with HDFC Ltd and under temporary NIM compression as the high-cost deposit book reprices. With RBI delivering rate cuts and loan growth re-accelerating toward 14% YoY in FY26, FY27 shapes up as a classic NIM recovery and re-rating year. Global institutions hold ~44% stake, a vote of confidence in long-run franchise value.

**Future potential:** As NIM normalises toward 3.8–4.0% and the merged balance sheet efficiencies show through, ROE can re-rate from current 13.8% toward 17–18%, justifying a 3.5–4x P/BV re-rating — implying 30–40% upside on a 12–18 month view. Credit card, mortgage and SME lending businesses are still underpenetrated.

**Key risk:** NIM recovery slower than expected; global macro slowdown hitting loan growth; any systemic NPA event in stressed sectors.

### #2 - HAL (NSE): Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd
**Sector:** Industrials / Aerospace & Defence | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR 2,92,148 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** HAL is the sole domestic manufacturer of combat aircraft and helicopters for the Indian Armed Forces. Despite a record 7-year order book (LCH helicopters, Su-30MKI engines, Tejas fighters) and Q4 FY26 earnings exceeding estimates, the stock has corrected ~15% from its 52-week high, offering a tactical entry. The government's record defence capital outlay in Budget 2026 and a fast-growing export pipeline provide multi-year earnings visibility.

**Future potential:** Defence indigenisation mandates mean HAL is in a secular demand cycle with virtually no private-sector competition on major platforms. Revenue is on track toward ₹50,000 Cr by FY28 and profit margins are expanding on higher-value products. Analyst mean target of ₹5,771 implies 32% upside from current levels.

**Key risk:** Programme delays on Tejas Mk1A deliveries; forex risks on imported components; concentration in a single government customer.
