# Daily India Stock Picks - 2026-05-27
**Analyst:** Claude (claude-sonnet-4-6) | **Exchange:** NSE & BSE | **Framework:** Growth + Quality + Valuation

## Market Context
The Nifty 50 has been staging a measured recovery through May 2026 after an 18-month SMID correction, buoyed by Budget 2026's record ₹11.1 lakh crore capex allocation, two successive RBI rate cuts totalling 50 bps since February, and renewed FPI inflows as India's Q4 FY26 earnings season delivered broadly in-line results. Institutional consensus (Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, HSBC) targets Nifty at 27,200 by June 2026 and 28,850 by year-end, suggesting a window for selective accumulation in quality names still trading below historical valuation multiples.

### #1 - HDFCBANK (NSE): HDFC Bank Ltd
**Sector:** Financials — Private Banking | **Cap:** Large Cap (₹11,68,659 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** HDFC Bank is trading at just 15.4x trailing earnings and 2.0x P/BV — well below its historical 5-year average of 20–25x PE and 3.5–4x P/BV. The stock has been in a prolonged re-rating trough post the HDFC Ltd merger as investors waited for NIM normalisation and balance sheet digestion to play out. That cycle is now firmly behind the bank: Q4 FY26 net profit rose 8% YoY to ₹20,351 Cr and full-year profit grew 7.4% to ₹76,026 Cr, with loan growth returning to 14% YoY in Q3.

**Future potential:** RBI's rate-cut cycle reduces funding costs while the bank's dominant 37% share of private-sector advances and a re-activation of its retail lending engine provide both earnings visibility and re-rating upside. Analysts expect FY27 to be the year of multiple expansion back toward the 20x band.

**Key risk:** Persistent NIM compression if rate cuts are faster than repricing; prolonged slowdown in the home-loan segment.

### #2 - HAL (NSE): Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd
**Sector:** Industrials — Defence & Aerospace | **Cap:** Large Cap (₹2,95,729 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** Despite a 7-year order-book underpinned by Tejas Mk1A, the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA), Prachand helicopters and LCA Navy programmes, HAL trades at 32x PE — a discount to global defence peers trading at 40–50x on similar growth visibility. The stock has corrected ~14% from its 52-week high of ₹5,147 as near-term revenue recognition slipped due to supply-chain delays, creating an attractive entry.

**Future potential:** Budget 2026's record defence capital outlay, India's push to achieve 70% domestic procurement by FY30, and a growing export pipeline to Southeast Asia & Africa provide a structural multi-decade growth story. Revenue from indigenous programmes is expected to step up sharply from FY27.

**Key risk:** Order execution slippage; government capex delays; geopolitical de-escalation reducing urgency of defence spend.

### #4 - ITC (NSE): ITC Ltd
**Sector:** Consumer — FMCG / Diversified | **Cap:** Large Cap (₹3,79,706 Cr) | **Conviction:** MEDIUM

**Why undervalued now:** ITC offers a rare combination of a cigarettes monopoly (wide moat, ~80% market share), a fast-scaling FMCG business, a thriving hotels segment and an agri-commodity business — all at just 18.6x PE with ROCE of 37.3% and zero debt. The stock has been in a consolidation phase as investors await faster FMCG margin expansion and the full spin-off benefit from ITC Hotels (listed separately in 2025).

**Future potential:** The FMCG segment (Sunfeast, Bingo, Savlon, Fiama) is approaching double-digit operating margins. ITC's hotels business benefits from a strong India travel cycle. Dividend payout of 74.5% makes it a compelling income-and-growth story. Analyst mean target of ₹369 implies ~22% upside.

**Key risk:** Cigarette excise hike beyond expectation; FMCG margin disappointment; slower hotels recovery.

### #6 - ONGC (NSE): Oil & Natural Gas Corporation Ltd
**Sector:** Energy — Oil & Gas E&P | **Cap:** Large Cap (₹3,60,677 Cr) | **Conviction:** MEDIUM

**Why undervalued now:** ONGC is one of India's deepest-value large-caps: P/E of 8.3x, P/BV of 1.01x and a dividend yield of 4.21% — a rare combination offering both income and capital appreciation. The stock has been weighed down by concerns over crude oil price volatility and government pricing interference, but with net debt declining (₹96,773 Cr as of Sep-25) and Q3 FY26 profit up 16.7% YoY to ₹10,016 Cr, earnings momentum is intact.

**Future potential:** India's energy security push, rising domestic natural gas pricing under the APM framework, and ONGC's KG basin deep-water ramp-up in FY27 provide earnings catalysts. **Key risk:** Crude oil price collapse; government windfall tax reimposition; under-investment relative to global peers creating long-term production decline.

### #7 - BAJFINANCE (NSE): Bajaj Finance Ltd
**Sector:** Financials — NBFC / Consumer Lending | **Cap:** Large Cap (₹5,77,302 Cr) | **Conviction:** MEDIUM

**Why undervalued now:** Post its 2025 stock split, Bajaj Finance trades at ₹940 with a P/E of 30.4x — a meaningful discount to its 3-year historical average of 40–50x PE. The stock has underperformed as investors worried about asset quality in the consumer credit cycle and rising cost of funds. FY26 results have dispelled those fears: net profit up 14.3% YoY to ₹19,017 Cr and NII growing 21% YoY to ₹44,110 Cr, with credit costs stabilising.

**Future potential:** With 101+ million customers, 4,000+ branches and diversified lending across retail, SME and commercial, Bajaj Finance has the deepest last-mile consumer credit moat in India. ROE of 19.6% is strong for an NBFC. Promoter holding of 54.7% with zero pledging is a key governance positive. As rate-cut cycle reduces borrowing costs, NIM expansion should drive a re-rating.

**Key risk:** Unsecured consumer credit stress; rising competition from large banks and digital NBFCs; regulatory tightening of credit card/personal loan limits.

### #8 - CIPLA (NSE): Cipla Ltd
**Sector:** Healthcare — Pharmaceuticals | **Cap:** Large Cap (₹1,14,235 Cr) | **Conviction:** MEDIUM

**Why undervalued now:** Cipla has corrected ~16% from its 52-week high of ₹1,673 to ₹1,399, as investors re-rated the stock on fears of US FDA import alerts and generic pricing pressure in the US market. **Key risk:** US FDA import alerts on key plants; intensified competition in the US from biosimilar and complex generics entrants; patent cliff on key branded products.
