## Market Context
Indian equities have shown divergent performance in 2026: the Nifty 50 is down ~8.85% YTD amid global macro headwinds, while the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 have delivered +1.28% and +4.58% respectively, reflecting a rotation toward quality mid-caps with resilient earnings. Domestic institutional investors have countered FPI outflows with ~$77 billion in net purchases, underpinning market stability and creating selective entry points in fundamentally strong names.

## Top 10 Undervalued Indian Stocks

### #1 - HDFCBANK (NSE): HDFC Bank Ltd
**Sector:** Banking & Financial Services | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR ~13,82,000 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** Post-merger integration with HDFC Ltd has created a temporary drag on RoA and deposit growth metrics, pushing the stock to a rare discount to its 5-year average P/B of ~3.5x. Current P/B of ~2.8x is at a multi-year low for India's most profitable private lender, while credit quality remains pristine (GNPA <1.5%).

**Future potential:** As the merger synergies fully materialise through FY27 — particularly cross-selling home loans via the bank's 9,100+ branch network and repricing of the HDFC Ltd mortgage book — consensus expects RoA to recover to ~2% and EPS CAGR of 17-19% over FY26-28. The stock has a history of re-rating sharply once integration concerns recede.

**Key risk:** Prolonged deposit cost pressure or any asset-quality deterioration in the unsecured retail book could delay re-rating.

### #2 - TCS (NSE): Tata Consultancy Services Ltd
**Sector:** Information Technology | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR ~12,65,000 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** Macro-driven IT spending caution and a slower US BFSI ramp have compressed TCS's forward P/E to ~24x — the lowest since 2020 — despite the company holding the highest revenue visibility in the sector via a $60+ billion order backlog. FY26 revenue growth of ~4-5% is well below its potential and priced as structural when it is cyclical.

**Future potential:** The ramp-up of large AI-led transformation deals (GenAI services book grew 40% YoY in Q4FY26), a recovery in discretionary BFSI spend in H2 CY26, and TCS's industry-leading 25%+ EBIT margins set the stage for 13-15% EPS CAGR in FY27-29. The company's $6+ billion annual free cash flow and consistent 80-90% dividend payout add to total-return appeal.

**Key risk:** Prolonged US recession or acceleration of in-house AI adoption by large clients could reduce outsourcing volumes.

### #3 - SUNPHARMA (NSE): Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd
**Sector:** Healthcare & Pharmaceuticals | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR ~4,12,000 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** Sun Pharma's US specialty portfolio (Ilumya, Winlevi, Levulan) is gaining market share rapidly, yet the stock trades at a ~20% discount to global specialty pharma peers on an EV/EBITDA basis. Near-term US pricing pressure and a high R&D spend phase have suppressed reported margins, creating an entry window.

**Future potential:** The dermatology specialty pipeline has 8+ late-stage candidates, and global specialty revenue is projected to cross $2 billion by FY28 (up from ~$1.2 billion in FY26). India domestic business — the largest branded generic franchise — continues growing at 11-12% annually. Management has guided to margin expansion of 200-300 bps over two years as R&D capitalisation peaks.

**Key risk:** USFDA inspectional risk at key manufacturing sites and potential biosimilar competition to flagship products.

### #4 - BEL (NSE): Bharat Electronics Ltd
**Sector:** Defence & Industrials | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR ~2,27,000 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** BEL has corrected ~25% from its 52-week high as defence capex disbursements slowed in Q3-Q4FY26 pending Union Budget clarity. However, the company's order book stands at a record ~INR 75,000 Cr (3.5x revenue), with 80%+ sourced domestically — directly aligned with India's Atmanirbhar Bharat defence indigenisation mandate.

**Future potential:** India's defence budget is set to grow at 10-12% annually through FY30, and BEL — as the primary electronics systems integrator for the armed forces — is the clearest beneficiary. New verticals including space electronics, EV power electronics, and homeland security are emerging as incremental growth levers. Management targets ~15% revenue CAGR through FY28.

**Key risk:** Execution delays in large orders (Akash missile systems, electronic warfare) and geopolitical de-escalation reducing urgent procurement timelines.

### #5 - ITC (NSE): ITC Ltd
**Sector:** Consumer / FMCG | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR ~5,60,000 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** Following the demerger of ITC Hotels (listed separately in early 2025), standalone ITC's FMCG + cigarettes + agri-business trades at a notable conglomerate discount. The core cigarettes business — a virtual monopoly with 80%+ market share — generates ~INR 16,000 Cr in annual EBIT, yet the stock's blended P/E of ~22x is below FMCG peers despite superior cash generation and 70%+ dividend payout.

**Future potential:** ITC's FMCG segment (Sunfeast, Classmate, Fiama, Aashirvaad) is on a path to 15%+ EBIT margins by FY28 (currently ~9%), which — combined with cigarette volume recovery post-GST rationalisation — could unlock a significant earnings re-rating. The agri-tech platform (e-Choupal 4.0) and digital ventures add optionality.

**Key risk:** Regulatory risk from further cigarette excise hikes or plain-packaging mandates; FMCG margin recovery timeline extending beyond expectations.

### #6 - AXISBANK (NSE): Axis Bank Ltd
**Sector:** Banking & Financial Services | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR ~3,64,000 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** Axis Bank trades at ~1.8x FY27E P/B versus ICICI Bank at ~3x and HDFC Bank at ~2.8x, despite delivering comparable RoE of ~17-18%. Post-Citi acquisition integration headwinds have largely passed, and the retail credit book is now growing 14-15% YoY with improving cost ratios. **Key risk:** Any stress in the unsecured personal loan or credit card book in a slower economic environment; management continuity.

### #7 - M&M (NSE): Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd
**Sector:** Automotive | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR ~3,62,000 Cr) | **Conviction:** HIGH

**Why undervalued now:** M&M's SUV franchise is at peak dominance — its order book for XUV700, Scorpio-N, and new BE-series electric SUVs (BE6, XEV9e) remains robust at 200,000+ units — yet the stock has retraced ~15% from highs on concerns about EV margin dilution and farm equipment seasonality. The market is discounting a prolonged EV investment phase that management has already guided will turn EBIT-positive by FY27.

**Future potential:** M&M is the only Indian OEM with a credible EV platform strategy (born-electric architecture), a growing global tractor presence (US, EU), and a financial services arm (Mahindra Finance) at an inflection point. Consensus projects 18-20% EPS CAGR over FY26-28 as EV volumes scale and tractor demand recovers on a normal monsoon cycle.

**Key risk:** Execution risk on EV ramp-up; competition from Tata Motors and new EV entrants; commodity cost volatility.

### #8 - APOLLOHOSP (NSE): Apollo Hospitals Enterprise Ltd
**Sector:** Healthcare | **Cap:** Large Cap (INR ~1,02,000 Cr) | **Conviction:** MEDIUM

**Why undervalued now:** Apollo Hospitals has dipped ~18% from its peak as the market digests near-term margin pressure from heavy investments in the Apollo HealthCo digital health platform and new hospital bed additions (~3,000 beds in FY26). However, mature bed occupancy in existing hospitals is running at 70%+ and ARPOB (Average Revenue Per Occupied Bed) is growing at 8-10% annually.

**Future potential:** Apollo's integrated healthcare ecosystem — hospitals, pharmacy retail (Apollo Pharmacy: 6,000+ stores), diagnostics, and digital health (Apollo 24|7 with 50M+ registered users) — is unique in India. As new beds mature (typically ~18-24 months to EBITDA breakeven), margin recovery and exponential free cash flow growth are expected from FY28. The healthcare sector's structural underpenetration in India makes this a decade-long compounder.

**Key risk:** Continued heavy capex investment cycle depressing near-term FCF; regulatory changes in hospital pricing; key-man risk.

### #9 - CANFINHOME (NSE): Can Fin Homes Ltd
**Sector:** Housing Finance / NBFC | **Cap:** Mid Cap (INR ~11,200 Cr) | **Conviction:** MEDIUM

**Why undervalued now:** Can Fin Homes — a Canara Bank subsidiary — trades at just 1.7x FY27E P/B, a steep 40-50% discount to larger housing finance peers like HDFC Ltd or LIC Housing Finance, despite delivering consistent 18-20% AUM growth, a sub-1% GNPA ratio, and RoE of ~19%. The discount is largely attributable to its lower liquidity and less institutional coverage, not fundamentals.

**Future potential:** India's housing finance market is expected to double in size by 2030, driven by urbanisation, the PM Awas Yojana (urban housing scheme), and rising disposable incomes. Can Fin's focus on the affordable and mid-segment home loan space (average ticket size ~INR 18 lakh) gives it access to the fastest-growing mortgage segment with government credit-linked subsidy tailwinds. Analysts see 25%+ upside as the valuation discount narrows.

**Key risk:** Any deterioration in parent Canara Bank's capitalisation could tighten Can Fin's borrowing costs; interest rate hikes compressing NIMs.

### #10 - GODAWARIPOW (NSE): Godawari Power & Ispat Ltd
**Sector:** Power / Steel / Industrials | **Cap:** Small Cap (INR ~7,500 Cr) | **Conviction:** SPECULATIVE

**Why undervalued now:** Godawari Power trades at just 6-7x FY27E EV/EBITDA — a significant discount to listed peers like NMDC Steel and Jindal Steel — despite operating a fully integrated iron ore pellet and DRI-to-steel chain in Chhattisgarh with one of the lowest cost structures in Indian steelmaking. A soft steel price cycle and temporary high coking coal costs in FY26 have compressed margins, creating a cyclical trough entry point.

**Future potential:** The company is executing a 1.5 MTPA capacity expansion (commissioned FY27) and adding a 200 MW captive power plant that will further reduce its energy cost advantage. With normalised steel spreads and new capacity, EBITDA is projected to grow 60-70% by FY28. Additionally, GPIL holds significant iron ore mining leases providing long-term raw material security — a competitive moat rare in Indian mid-cap steel.

**Key risk:** Global steel oversupply, particularly Chinese dumping; commodity cycle downturn; higher-than-expected capex cost overruns; low liquidity in the stock.
